## Methodology

- **Primary preference:** Official athletics coaching directories, roster/coaching-staff blocks, and school media guides for the 2026 season.
- **Cross-checks:** Wikipedia 2026 team pages and the FBS coaches compilation; CBS Sports / ESPN coordinator-hire coverage; credible beat and national reporting (e.g., Sports Illustrated scheme surveys, Clarion Ledger, Capital-Journal, Deseret News, Daily Illini).
- **Conflict handling:** When athletics CMS pages, roster PDFs, Wikipedia, and beat reporting disagree, official athletics directories are preferred for on-field titles; material conflicts are noted in the conference completeness sections (examples: Mississippi State co-DC sourcing; Washington special-teams title; Minnesota co-OC Wikipedia vs. official table).
- **“Not listed” standard:** Applied when no reliable 2026 primary source named a discrete role or a formal scheme / base-front label. Historical résumé language or flexible/multiple descriptions are not converted into a single invented base tag.
- **Access / research window:** On or about **September 5, 2026 (PT)**. Midseason staff changes after that date are outside this snapshot.

## Common gaps (cross-conference)

These patterns appear repeatedly in the four source reports and should be treated as structural limits of the public record, not as omissions of available names:

1. **Special teams titles**  
   Dedicated STC titles are generally complete in the SEC and Big Ten snapshots, but gaps and title ambiguity remain elsewhere — e.g., **Colorado** (no dedicated STC on the official 2026 coaches directory; GA / special-teams listing only), **TCU** (official title Special Assistant vs. beat preview STC), and **Washington** (Wikipedia vs. official UW directory disagreement; official directory preferred). Several STCs are dual-titled with position rooms.

2. **Dual-role offensive coordinators (and HC play-callers)**  
   Many programs publish OC / Quarterbacks (or Co-OC / QBs) as a single title rather than separate rooms — especially common in the Big 12 and Pac-12 source tables. A smaller set has **no separate titled OC**: **Mississippi State** (HC Jeff Lebby oversees offense), **West Virginia** (Rich Rodriguez runs the offense; no titled OC), and **Washington** (Jedd Fisch calls plays; no separate titled OC). Dual titles are reported as stated, not artificially split.

3. **Flexible defensive fronts often unlabeled**  
   Where staffs describe multiple, hybrid, or personnel-driven fronts without a single official “base defense” line, entries are **Not listed** (or carefully attributed as multiple). This is especially visible in large portions of the SEC and Big 12 defensive columns, and in many Big Ten programs outside a documented subset (e.g., Illinois 3-3-5, Indiana 4-2-5, Michigan 4-2-5, USC 4-2-5, and others noted in-conference). Pac-12 coverage is stronger for several nickel / 4-2-5 and 3-3-5 labels but still leaves some bases unlabeled.

Additional conference-specific gaps (QB room titles, Wikipedia lag, incomplete CMS fetches) are retained in each PART’s completeness section rather than restated here.

## Brief scheme themes (evident in sources only)

Themes below summarize language already present in the conference files. No new scheme assignments are added.

- **SEC:** Sports Illustrated (2026) groups roughly seven SEC offenses with the **Veer & Shoot** (or close tempo / wide-split relatives), citing practitioners tied to Heupel, Lebby, Briles, Kiffin, Golesh, Cramsey, and Baker among others — with noted label blur when coaches reject rigid media tagging (e.g., Golesh). Documented defensive bases include examples such as Alabama **4–2–5**, Georgia **3–4**, and Tennessee Knowles **4–2–5**; many other SEC defenses remain **Not listed** as a single official base.
- **Big 12:** Published offensive identities include Air Raid–influenced / spread continuity (e.g., Arizona, Oklahoma State), Colorado’s **Go-Go**, and various multiple / tempo descriptions. Clear 2026 defensive base tags in-source include Cincinnati **3-4**, Iowa State **4-2-5**, and TCU Avalos **4-2-5**; exclusivity of a single front remains **Not listed** for a substantial share of the league.
- **Big Ten:** Offensive language spans pro-style / multiple, RPO-heavy, Shanahan / wide-zone, tempo-spacing, and air-raid / spread continuity where sourced. Defensive fronts are strongly labeled for a subset (e.g., Illinois **3-3-5**, Indiana **4-2-5**, Michigan **4-2-5**, Nebraska / Penn State / USC as noted in-conference); many others are multiple / attacking without a published exclusive base.
- **Pac-12 (8-team rebuild roster):** A run-first / physical offensive cohort (Boise State, Colorado State, Fresno State) sits alongside **Veer-and-Shoot** lineage at San Diego State and Texas State, plus tempo / spread-rush language at Washington State. Defensive labels in-source include several **4-2-5** / nickel identities and Utah State **3-3-5**; Colorado State and Washington State emphasize multiple / hybrid fronts without a single static base.
